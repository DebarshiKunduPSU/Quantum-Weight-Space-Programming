**Design idea.** In these 8×8 digits the "9" and "4" differ most reliably in two regions:

- **Bottom-left (rows 6–7, columns 2–3: x[50], x[51], x[58], x[59])**: the tail of a 9 curves left and lights these pixels; a 4's lower stroke sits at columns 4–5, leaving them dark.
- **Left side of the middle (rows 3–5, column 1: x[25], x[33], x[41])**: the left arm of a 4 descends here before the crossbar; a 9's loop stays at columns 2–6, so these are dark.

I use a single qubit as a linear accumulator: rotations `ry` about the same axis simply add their angles, so the final state is `ry(pi/2 + s)|0>` with `s = Σ w_i x_i`. Then P(1) = sin²(π/4 + s/2), monotone increasing in s on the range the weights can reach (|s| < π). Positive weights go on the 9-evidence pixels, negative on the 4-evidence pixels, so outcome 1 → class 9, outcome 0 → class 4.

**Design idea.** A 9 has a closed loop at the top (bright pixels in row 1, columns 2–3: x[10], x[11]) and its tail curves to the lower-left, giving ink in the bottom row at columns 1–3 (x[57], x[58], x[59]). A 4 instead has a horizontal crossbar in rows 4–5 that extends to the left (x[33], x[34], x[41], x[42]), a region that is dark for a 9, and its bottom stroke sits only at column 4. So I build a linear score s = (9-evidence) − (4-evidence) and feed it into a single-qubit RY rotation: successive RY gates on one qubit simply add their angles, so the total angle is θ = π/2 + 0.6·s. For a 4, s ≈ −1.5 → θ ≈ 0.6 → P(1) small; for a 9, s ≈ +1.8 → θ ≈ 2.7 → P(1) large. Measuring |1⟩ predicts 9, |0⟩ predicts 4.

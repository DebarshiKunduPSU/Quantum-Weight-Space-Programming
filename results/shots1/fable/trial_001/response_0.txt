**Design idea.** In 8×8 digits the clearest difference between a 3 and an 8 is the left side of the middle rows: an 8 closes both loops, so pixels in columns 1–2 of rows 2–5 carry ink, whereas a 3 is open on the left and those pixels are dark. A 3 instead tends to push its middle stroke to the right (row 3, column 5). I accumulate these pixels as commuting RY rotations on a single qubit (so the total angle is a linear score), with positive weights on the left-middle pixels, a negative weight on the right-middle pixel, and a constant offset acting as the bias. Weights are scaled so the total angle stays in [0, π] over the realistic feature range, keeping P(|1⟩)=sin²(θ/2) monotone in the score. Qubit measured 1 → class 8, 0 → class 3.

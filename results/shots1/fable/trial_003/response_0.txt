**Design idea.** The main difference between a 3 and an 8 at 8×8 resolution is ink on the *left* side of the image: an 8 closes both loops, so pixels in columns 1–2 at rows 2–3 (upper loop) and rows 5–6 (lower loop) are bright, whereas a 3 is open on the left and those pixels are dark (apart from an occasional tail near row 6, col 2, which gets a smaller weight). 

Since consecutive RY rotations on one qubit add their angles, a single qubit computes a linear score: θ = bias + Σ wᵢ·x[i] over those eight left-side pixels. The bias is set so that θ ≈ π/2 at the decision threshold (score ≈ 0.45 in weighted units); P(|1⟩) = sin²(θ/2) is then > 0.5 for 8-like images and < 0.5 for 3-like images. Weights are scaled so θ stays roughly within [0, π] over the realistic feature range.

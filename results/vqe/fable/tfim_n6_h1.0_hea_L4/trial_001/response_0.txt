**Approach.** The CX ladder maps a computational basis state |s⟩ to its cumulative parities |t⟩, so the original bits become the domain-wall indicators of the new state. Hence a product state ⊗ ry(a_i)|0⟩ followed by one ladder gives a Jastrow-type wavefunction ψ(t) ∝ ∏ w(t_{i-1},t_i) with w = cos(a_i/2) for aligned and sin(a_i/2) for anti-aligned neighbours — a classical-Ising-like ferromagnetic correlation, exactly the right structure for the TFIM ground state (real, positive amplitudes, Z₂-symmetric). For this state one finds ⟨Z_{i-1}Z_i⟩ = cos a_i, ⟨X_0⟩ = sin a_1, ⟨X_5⟩ = sin a_5, ⟨X_i⟩ = sin a_i sin a_{i+1} in the bulk. Minimizing E = −2cos b − 3cos a − 2sin b − 2 sin a sin b − 2 sin²a over a bulk angle a and edge angle b gives a ≈ 1.05, b ≈ 1.08, E ≈ −7.23 (exact ≈ −7.30). I set the first three RY layers to zero so the first three ladders act trivially on |000000⟩, put the Jastrow angles in layer 4 (θ[18]=π/2 to make the first spin unbiased), and set the final layer to zero since small final rotations only raise the energy by symmetry.

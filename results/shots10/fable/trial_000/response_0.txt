**Design idea.** The clearest difference between a 3 and an 8 on an 8×8 grid is the left side of the glyph in the middle rows: an 8 closes both loops on the left (ink in columns 1–2 at rows 2–5), whereas a 3 is open on the left there (those pixels are nearly black; only the junction near column 2–3 at rows 3–4 occasionally has faint ink). So I build a linear "left-side ink" detector from pixels x[17], x[18], x[25], x[26], x[33], x[34], x[41], x[42], weighting column 1 and the outer rows (2 and 5) more, since those are most reliably dark for a 3.

Sequential RY rotations on one qubit add their angles, so the circuit implements θ = −1.2 + Σ wᵢxᵢ and P(|1⟩) = sin²(θ/2). The bias −1.2 puts the decision boundary at Σ wᵢxᵢ ≈ 1.2: a 3 (near-zero left-side ink) stays near |0⟩, an 8 (sum typically 2–3) rotates toward |1⟩, while even the maximal sum (~4.5) keeps θ < 2π−π/2, so no wrap-around errors.

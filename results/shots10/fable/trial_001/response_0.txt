**Design idea.** In 8×8 digit images the clearest difference between 3 and 8 is the left side of the middle rows: an 8 has ink on the left of both loops (columns 1–2, rows 2–5), whereas a 3 is open on the left there (its strokes only touch the left in the top and bottom rows). So a linear score built from those left‑middle pixels separates the classes well.

A single qubit suffices: successive `ry` rotations on the same qubit add their angles, so a chain of `ry(w_i·x[i])` gates implements `ry(c + Σ w_i x[i])`, and P(|1⟩) = sin²(total/2) exceeds 0.5 exactly when the total angle passes π/2. Weights are chosen so a typical 3 gives a total ≈ 0.5–1.0 rad (predict 3), a typical 8 gives ≈ 2–3.5 rad (predict 8), and the maximum possible total (4.4 rad) stays below 3π/2 so the decision stays monotone.

**Design idea.** In 8×8 digit images, the clearest difference between 3 and 8 is the lower-left region: an 8 has the left side of its lower loop (rows 4–6, columns 1–2) inked, while a 3 is open on the left there (its lower bowl sits on the right). So I build a one-qubit linear-threshold classifier: successive RY rotations on a single qubit add their angles, so P(1) = sin²(θ_total/2) with θ_total = bias + Σ w_i x_i. Weights are positive on the lower-left pixels (x[33], x[34], x[41], x[42], x[49], x[50]); the bias puts the decision boundary (θ_total = π/2) at a feature sum ≈ 1.5, which separates "3" (sum near 0–0.8) from "8" (sum near 2.5–4). Measuring 1 → class 8, 0 → class 3.

**Design idea.** The difference between a 3 and an 8 in these 8×8 images is the left side of the two loops: an 8 closes its loops with ink in the left columns (cols 1–2) around rows 2 and 5 (and some at rows 3–4 where the loops cross), while a 3 is open on the left, so those pixels are dark. Top/bottom rows are avoided because the 3's horizontal strokes can reach the left there.

I use a single qubit. Successive RY rotations about the same axis add up, so the circuit computes θ = c + Σ wᵢ x[i] and P(|1⟩) = sin²(θ/2). The bias puts the decision boundary (θ = π/2) at a weighted left-side ink sum of ≈0.6: typical 3 → θ≈1.1 (P₁≈0.28), typical 8 → θ≈2.6 (P₁≈0.93), with the maximum possible θ staying below π so the mapping is monotone.

**Analysis of what q0 can "see"**

Working in the Heisenberg picture, q0 is never a CNOT target, so after pulling the final Z₀ back through the circuit only row-qubit (q0,q1,q2) operators appear; the column qubits never influence the readout. The available features are therefore row-profile Z-strings (Z₀, Z₁, Z₀Z₁, Z₀Z₂, …) and same-column, row-to-row correlations (X₀ ↔ pixel (r,c)·pixel(r+4,c), etc.).

Two row-based features separate 3 from 8 reasonably:
- ⟨Z₀Z₁⟩ = ink in rows {0,1,6,7} − ink in rows {2,3,4,5}: a "3" has very bright top and bottom bars and only a thin stroke in the middle rows (≈ +0.2), while an "8" has two strokes in every middle row (≈ +0.05–0.1).
- ⟨X₀⟩ = correlation between row r and row r+4 in the same column: an "8" has long vertical strokes and vertically aligned arcs/crossing (≈ 0.5–0.7), a "3" much less (≈ 0.3–0.4).

**Realizing O = s₆·Z₀Z₁ + c₆·X₀**

With θ₁₂=0 the observable is Z₀ → (after ry(θ₆) and the ladder) c₆Z₀ − s₆X₀X₁. Choosing θ₀=−π/2 (Z₀→X₀, X₀→−Z₀) and θ₁=π/2 (X₁→Z₁) gives exactly O = c₆X₀ + s₆Z₀Z₁. Picking θ₆ ≈ 1.92 (s₆≈0.94, c₆≈−0.34) gives P(q0=1) = (1 − 0.94⟨Z₀Z₁⟩ + 0.34⟨X₀⟩)/2, so an image is called "8" when 0.37·⟨X₀⟩ > ⟨Z₀Z₁⟩ — satisfied for the sketched 8s (≈0.25 vs 0.1) and violated for the 3s (≈0.13 vs 0.2). All other angles are zero (θ₁₃–θ₁₇ do not affect q0 at all).

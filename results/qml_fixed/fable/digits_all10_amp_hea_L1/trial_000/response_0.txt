**Approach.** With only 12 angles on a CNOT-chain circuit, the most controllable thing is *which groups of pixels* each readout bitstring collects. I use:

- `ry(-π/2)` on q0 (row bit r2): this coherently folds the top and bottom halves of the image (rows r and r+4 are summed), so q0 ends up ≈ 0 and the remaining three readout bits become a pooled "row-pair × column-half" feature map, which is more robust than single rows.
- `ry(π)` on q1 (flip r1) to permute which pooled row-pair goes to which class pair, so that (after the CNOT chain) class 0/1 ↔ rows {2,6} left/right, class 2/3 ↔ rows {3,7} right/left, class 4/5 ↔ rows {1,5} left/right, class 6/7 ↔ rows {0,4} right/left. This roughly matches where ink mass concentrates: "1" has its stroke in the right half of the middle rows, "5" has its top bar / bottom hook in the right of rows 1 and 5, "7" has top-bar mass in rows 0/4, "0" / "6" have left-side strokes in rows 2/6, "2"/"3" have mass in rows 3/7.
- All other angles 0 so the CNOT chain acts as a pure relabelling of basis states and no uncontrolled interference is introduced.
